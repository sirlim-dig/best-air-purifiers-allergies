1. Understanding HEPA Filtration

HEPA stands for High Efficiency Particulate Air. To qualify as "True HEPA," a filter must capture 99.97% of particles at 0.3 microns. This is the gold standard for allergy relief.

⚠️ Watch out for "HEPA-type" or "HEPA-style" filters

These marketing terms mean the filter does NOT meet the true HEPA standard. They may only capture 85-90% of particles. Always look for "True HEPA" or "H13 HEPA" certification.

5. What to Avoid

❌ Ozone Generators

Marketed as "air purifiers" but produce ozone, which is a lung irritant and makes allergies WORSE. The EPA warns against these.

❌ Ionic/Electrostatic Only

These charge particles to stick to surfaces but don't actually remove them from your air. You're just moving allergens to your walls.

❌ UV-C Only Purifiers

UV light kills germs but does nothing for allergens like pollen, dust, or dander. It's a nice add-on, not a replacement for HEPA.

❌ "HEPA-type" Filters

Not regulated. Could be capturing 80% or 95% — you don't know. Only buy True HEPA (H13 or H14) certified units.

6. Optimal Placement Tips

  • ✅ Place in your bedroom — you spend 8 hours there and allergen reduction during sleep has the biggest impact on symptoms
  • ✅ Keep at least 3 feet from walls and furniture for proper airflow
  • ✅ Place near allergen sources (pet beds, windows that open, entryways)
  • ✅ Keep doors closed in the room where your purifier runs
  • ❌ Don't place in corners — they need open space to circulate air
  • ❌ Don't place behind furniture or in closets
